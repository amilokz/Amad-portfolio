How the size is worked out

For a round bottle or can, the label width comes from the circumference — and the circumference comes from the diameter: Circumference = Diameter × π (3.1416). A full wrap is the circumference minus a 3 mm gap so the ends never collide, or plus a 6 mm overlap if the seam should overlap itself. A front label is usually about 45% of the circumference. The label height is the usable straight height you measured. Add bleed to both sides of each dimension: a label with 3 mm bleed needs 6 mm extra in each direction for the artwork file.

How to measure a bottle or jar

Measure the straight section only — from where the shoulder starts to flatten to where the base begins to taper. Measure the diameter across the widest straight part, or wrap a strip of paper around the container, mark where it meets, and measure it flat. Never measure the shoulder or the base curves: a flat label will not sit there. A small gap (about 3 mm) hides small measuring and application errors.

Frequently asked questions

How do I measure a bottle for a label?

Measure the straight section only — from where the shoulder starts to flatten to where the base begins to taper. Measure the diameter across the widest straight part, or wrap a strip of paper around it, mark where it meets, and measure it flat. Never measure the shoulder or the base curves: a flat label will not sit there.

What is the label size formula?

Circumference = diameter × π (3.1416). A full wrap label is the circumference minus a small gap (3 mm), or plus an overlap for the seam. A front label is usually about 45% of the circumference. Add bleed — usually 3 mm on every side — to get the artwork size.

Do I need bleed on a label?

Yes — 3 mm on every side is the standard. The artwork must extend past the cut line so the cutter’s small drift never leaves a white edge.
